That Nazarene — Uncle Theology (Official Lyric Video)

The risen Carpenter King who drew near — His risen wounds curing all doubt; He knows my name, my hairs, my tomorrows, and death itself is just an empty tomb.

📖 Scripture: John 20:24–29 · Mark 6:3 · Matthew 10:30 · 1 Corinthians 15:54–57

🔑 In this song:
• That Nazarene / Carpenter King — Jesus of Nazareth, the despised backwater town (John 1:46, "Can anything good come out of Nazareth?"). "Carpenter" recalls Mark 6:3, where His own townsfolk sneer, "Is not this the carpenter?" The Greek is tektōn (τέκτων) — a craftsman who builds in wood or stone, a manual laborer. The song takes that dismissal and crowns it: the working-man's hands are the King's hands.
• His risen wounds are curin all my doubt'n — The doubting-Thomas scene of John 20:24-29. Thomas refuses to believe unless he can put his finger in the nail-marks and his hand in Jesus' side; the risen Christ offers him exactly that, and Thomas answers, "My Lord and my God." The point the song presses: the resurrection body still bears its scars, and it is the wounds — not a wound-free glory — that heal unbelief.
• He knows my name, my hairs and my tomorrows — Matthew 10:30, "But the very hairs of your head are all numbered." In context Jesus is telling the disciples not to fear those who can kill the body — the meticulous care of God (counting hairs) is the ground for fearlessness before death, which is exactly where the lyric turns next ("death itself is just an empty tomb").

💬 Discussion: The song keeps repeating "I don't know how they live their lives without him" — how they face sorrow, the empty room, the world unbroken. How should Christian wonder at unbelief express itself: as compassion, as evangelistic urgency, as gratitude, or all three? What does it become if that wonder curdles into condescension?
