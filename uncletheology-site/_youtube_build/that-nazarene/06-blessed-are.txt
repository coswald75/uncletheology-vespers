Blessed Are — Uncle Theology (Official Lyric Video)

Isaac Watts's hymn on the Beatitudes, set to soaring shoegaze — the low ones rise, empty hands are the hands that hold, and through shame and flame comes glory and gold.

📖 Scripture: Matthew 5:3–12 · Luke 6:20–23

🔑 In this song:
• Blessed (makarios) — The word that opens every Beatitude is the Greek makarios — not a wish ("may you be happy") but a declaration of a settled, God-given flourishing. Jesus is not prescribing how to earn favor; He is announcing who already stands under it. The song's repeated "Blessed, blessed" is this pronouncement made audible. (Matthew 5:3)
• Poor in spirit / "empty hands are the hands that hold" — Matthew's "poor in spirit" translates ptōchos, the destitute beggar who has nothing of his own — not the modestly needy but the utterly bankrupt. Luke sharpens it to "blessed are you poor" (Luke 6:20). Chris's chorus line captures the paradox exactly: the kingdom is grasped only by hands that have stopped clutching and admit they are empty. (Matthew 5:3; Luke 6:20)
• Isaac Watts & the 1707 hymn — "Blessed Are the Humble Souls That See" is Isaac Watts's metrical paraphrase of the Beatitudes, published in his landmark 1707 collection Hymns and Spiritual Songs. Watts — often called the father of English hymnody — pioneered singing freely composed devotional verse rather than only metrical psalms. The song is his eight-stanza walk through Matthew 5, with Oswald adding new choruses around Watts's own lines.

💬 Discussion: The Beatitudes describe a great reversal: the low ones rise and empty hands are the hands that hold. Where do you see our culture's map of who is "blessed" running exactly backwards from Jesus' — and where has the church quietly adopted the world's map instead?
