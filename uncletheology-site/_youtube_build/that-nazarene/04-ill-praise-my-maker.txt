I’ll Praise My Maker — Uncle Theology (Official Lyric Video)

Isaac Watts's paraphrase of Psalm 146, reworked — I'll praise my Maker while I've breath, and also after I've tasted death; my lungs were made to be a horn for His majesty.

📖 Scripture: Psalm 146 · Psalm 104:33 · Psalm 150:6

🔑 In this song:
• "I'll praise my Maker while I've breath" — Isaac Watts's 1719 paraphrase of Psalm 146:2 ("while I live will I praise the LORD"). The breath motif rhymes with Psalm 104:33 ("I will sing unto my God while I have my being") and Psalm 150:6 ("let every thing that hath breath praise the LORD").
• Isaac Watts (1674-1748) — The "father of English hymnody." This text comes from his Psalms of David Imitated in the Language of the New Testament (1719), where he deliberately re-voiced the Hebrew psalms through Christ rather than translating them literally.
• John Wesley's adaptation — Wesley abridged and reshaped Watts's hymn for Methodist use. By long tradition it was among the hymns on his lips as he was dying in 1791 — the vow to "praise after death" sung by a man at death's door.

💬 Discussion: Psalm 146 anchors praise in what God concretely does for the oppressed, the blind, the prisoner, and the widow. How should that shape what our worship songs are actually about?
