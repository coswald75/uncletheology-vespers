About this songThe everyday, non-saving grace God pours on the whole human race — sun on the sinner, rain on the saved — kindness meant to lead us to repentance.
Big ideaA warm gospel-soul celebration of common grace — the undeserved, non-saving kindness God pours on believer and unbeliever alike (sun, rain, talent, civic order) — that carefully refuses to let anyone mistake it for the rarer saving grace they still need.
Doctrine
Common grace; providence; restraint
Anchor text
Matthew 5:45
Form
Gospel-soul, call-and-response, with a preacher's talk-break
Voices
James (the epistle, ch. 1); the Westminster Confession (ch. 5, Providence)
Verse 1

Woke up this mornin', sun on my face,
didn't earn a drop of it — baby, that's grace.
Not the saving kind that writes my name in the Book,
just the everyday kind — go on, take a look.

Talk-Break / Bridge

Now hold up — don't you get it twisted, honey, listen close:
this ain't the grace that raises up a heart that's cold.
That's the special kind — the blood-bought, chosen embrace.
But while you're still walkin' around? He's holdin' your place.
Providence, y'all — Westminster, chapter five:
He upholds every atom just to keep you alive.

Final Chorus

So it's that com-mon grace (grace!)

buildin' up the cities, keepin' order in the streets —
kindness meant to lead ya to repentance at His feet.
It's that com-mon grace — but oh, don't stop there,

Com-mon, com-mon grace.

Study this song

Teaching aids drawn from the song — for personal study or group discussion.

How the song moves

1
Undeserved sunrise
Verse 1 wakes to "sun on my face" you "didn't earn a drop of," then draws the line at once: not "the saving kind that writes my name in the Book," just the everyday kind (Matthew 5:45).
2
Talent on loan
Verse 2 credits the unbeliever's masterpiece and the pagan's soul-soothing tune to "the Giver of the good gifts" — "every perfect present, James chapter one" — from the Father of the moon and sun (James 1:17).
3
The talk-break
The spoken bridge warns "don't get it twisted," separates common grace from "the special kind — the blood-bought, chosen embrace," and grounds it in providence: "Westminster, chapter five… He upholds every atom just to keep you alive" (WCF 5; Hebrews 1:3).
4
Don't stop there
The final chorus turns civic — restraining evil, "buildin' up the cities, keepin' order in the streets" — then reframes all of it as "kindness meant to lead ya to repentance," and sends you running for the saving kind, "'cause that one's rare" (Romans 2:4).

Key terms & allusions

  • common grace vs. saving grace — The Reformed distinction the whole song hangs on: common grace is God's undeserved goodness to all people (sun, rain, gifts, restraint), while saving (special) grace is the redeeming, name-in-the-Book work given only to the elect.
  • "sun on the sinner, rain on the saved" — A near-quotation of Matthew 5:45, where the Father "makes his sun rise on the evil and on the good, and sends rain on the just and on the unjust" — Jesus' own proof-text for indiscriminate divine kindness.
  • "Father of the moon and sun… no shadow, no turning" — James 1:17 — "every good gift… comes down from the Father of lights, with whom is no variation or shadow due to change" — making even a pagan's talent a gift on loan from an unchanging God.
  • "Westminster, chapter five" — The Westminster Confession of Faith's chapter On Providence, which teaches that God "upholds, directs, disposes, and governs" all creatures and things down to the smallest — the song's "every atom to keep you alive."
  • "kindness meant to lead ya to repentance" — Romans 2:4 — Paul warns against presuming on "the riches of his kindness," since "God's kindness is meant to lead you to repentance" — the hinge that turns common grace from a comfort into a summons.

Discussion questions

  1. Where do you see common grace at work in our city — in its art, its neighbors, its institutions — and how might naming it together change the way we talk about people who don't share our faith?
  2. The song insists that even the unbeliever's masterpiece comes from "the Father of lights" (James 1:17). How should that conviction shape the way our church engages culture, beauty, and the wider community?
  3. The bridge warns against "getting it twisted" — treating common grace as if it saved. How can we celebrate God's everyday kindness together without letting anyone drift into skipping the "rare" saving kind?

For contemplation

  1. When did you last notice a gift you "didn't earn a drop of" — and were you among "the folks who never say thank-you," or did you name the Giver (Matthew 5:45)?
  2. The final chorus calls common grace "kindness meant to lead ya to repentance." Where is God's patience toward you right now meant to be leading you (Romans 2:4)?
  3. Have you settled for the everyday grace of sun and rain while never "running to get the saving kind"? What is holding you back from it?
