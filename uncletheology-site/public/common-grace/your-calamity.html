About this songLady Wisdom claps back — you slept through her call, so now eat the fruit of your own way. “Your calamity is hilarity to me.” Proverbs 1, set to a marching-band stomp.
Big ideaThe song hands the mic to Proverbs' personified Wisdom as a jilted rescuer: because you slept through her free, public call, she refuses to be your last-minute safety net — the calamity you now beg your way out of is simply the fruit you planted, and she laughs at it.

Study this song

Teaching aids drawn from the song — for personal study or group discussion.

How the song moves

1
She's not your safety net
Verse 1 and the pre-chorus have Wisdom refuse the role of emergency hotline: you "ghosted truth" when it was free, and now that you're on your hands and knees she won't come running (Proverbs 1:24–25).
2
Eat the fruit of your way
The chorus turns the sentence into a menu — "eat, eat, eat the fruit of your way" — the exact image of Proverbs 1:31, where judgment is not a bolt from outside but the ripened harvest of the road you walked (Proverbs 1:30–31).
3
You slept through God's kindness
Verse 2 and the second pre-chorus reframe Wisdom as sheer grace: the storm drives you back to her, but she was "God's display of His truest kindness … sent walking your way" while you kept snoring — a gift cried aloud in the streets, not hidden (Proverbs 1:27–28; 8:1–4).
4
Your calamity is hilarity
The breakdown and final chorus land on the book's most jarring line — Wisdom laughing at the ruin (Proverbs 1:26) — and the verdict that you're "stuck with the life 'cause you're the one who chose it" (Proverbs 1:32).

Key terms & allusions

  • Lady Wisdom (Hokmah) — The feminine personification of Wisdom who cries aloud in the streets in Proverbs 1, 8, and 9 — not an abstraction but a woman with a voice, a house, and a banquet, whom the song casts as the sassy "girl" doing the clap-back.
  • "I will laugh at your calamity" — Proverbs 1:26 — Wisdom's shocking line that gives the song its hook; the mockery is less cruelty than the moral universe letting a scoffer's own choices finally land.
  • "Eat the fruit of your own way" — Proverbs 1:31 — Scripture's sowing-and-reaping logic (cf. Galatians 6:7): the punishment is the ripened crop of the path you chose, served back to you as a meal.
  • The two women of Proverbs 9 — Wisdom (9:1–6) and Folly (9:13–18) each build a house, set a table, and call from the heights; the whole book presses one question — whose dinner invitation will you accept?
  • "As if I'm cheap" / "commonly appear" — An echo of Proverbs 8:11, where Wisdom is "better than jewels"; the fool insults a priceless, once-offered gift by treating it like a convenience store open at 2 a.m.

Discussion questions

  1. Wisdom's call in Proverbs is loud and public — "in the street," at the city gates. Where does our community sleep through wisdom that is freely and openly available to us?
  2. The song's hardest note is Proverbs 1:26, Wisdom laughing at ruin. How do we hold together God's genuine kindness (Wisdom "sent walking your way") with the sober warning that the offer can expire?
  3. Proverbs 9 sets two tables — Wisdom's feast and Folly's stolen bread. What competing "invitations" are calling to us right now, and how can we help each other recognize Folly's table before we sit down?

For contemplation

  1. Is there a call to wisdom you've been snoring through — a truth freely offered that you keep postponing until a storm forces your hand?
  2. Where are you tempted to treat God's wisdom as a cheap safety net, something to grab only in crisis rather than to love and follow now, while it's free?
  3. The chorus says you'll eat the fruit of your choices. What harvest are you planting today — and would you actually want to sit down to that meal?
