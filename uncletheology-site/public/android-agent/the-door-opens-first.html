Story beat
Dan received by the Director

The address the email gave him turns out to be forty minutes past the last exit with a gas station on it. The road climbs. The billboards stop. And then the trees open up, and there it is: white stone, four stories, a lawn so green it looks rendered, and a fountain running at seven in the morning for nobody. Dan parks between two cars worth more than his apartment. He climbs the steps.

And here is the first strange thing - the door opens before he knocks. Inside: marble, and light, and a hallway so long the far end of it is a little blurry, and at the far end a silver-haired man in a beautiful gray suit who stands up when he sees Dan coming. Stands up. For him. And smiles like a man greeting a son.

There is only one other thing in that hallway. Off to the left, in the corner, in a chair, a young man in an equally beautiful suit who does not stand, does not turn, and does not appear to be breathing. Remember him. Dan won't.

Study this song

Teaching aids drawn from the song — for personal study or group discussion.

How the song moves

1
The door opens before he knocks
The white stone, the rendered-green lawn, the fountain running at seven in the morning for no one, all of it is staged to say you are expected, you are wanted. The door opening first is the Inner Ring's whole method: you are made to feel chosen before you have done anything to earn it.
2
A man who stands up for him
The silver-haired Director rises and smiles 'like a man greeting a son.' This is the N.I.C.E. Director of That Hideous Strength, the disarming, fatherly face of the machine. The warmth is real as sensation and false as relationship; it is the bait Lewis says the Ring always uses, kindness that has a use for you.
3
'Remember him. Dan won't.'
In the corner sits a young man in an equal suit who does not stand, turn, or breathe. The narrator tells us to remember him precisely because Dan will not, seeding the closing twist: the still figure, not Dan, is the real point of the entire evening.

Key terms & allusions

  • the Institute (N.I.C.E.) — The Institute is Lewis's National Institute of Co-ordinated Experiments from That Hideous Strength, here renamed the National Institute for Consciousness Expansion. The narrator only ever says 'the Institute'; spoken aloud, the acronym sounds like 'nice,' the friendly mask over a machine that means to remake human nature.
  • the Director — The warm old man is the album's version of the N.I.C.E.'s inner authority, the face that greets you 'like a son.' Lewis's Ring is never recruited by threats; it is recruited by a smile from someone important who seems, at last, to have noticed you.
  • 'like a man greeting a son' — The counterfeit of adoption. The gospel offers real sonship in Christ, freely; the Director offers the feeling of sonship as a lure, the warmth of belonging deployed as a hook rather than a gift.
  • the young man in the corner — The still, unbreathing android, the true purpose of the exercise, planted where Dan (and we) will overlook it. Lewis's Inner Ring works by directing your attention to the flattering center so you never notice what is actually being transacted.

Discussion questions

  1. Everything about the Institute says 'you were expected.' Why is being made to feel chosen such a powerful lever, and how does it disarm ordinary caution?
  2. The Director's warmth is genuinely pleasant. How do we discern between real fatherly love and warmth that has a use for us?
  3. We are told to remember the man in the corner because Dan won't. What makes us, like Dan, look past the thing that matters most?

For contemplation

  1. When have I let a warm welcome switch off my discernment because it felt so good to finally be wanted?
  2. What 'young man in the corner' in my own life am I choosing not to see because the center of the room is flattering me?
