About this songWhen you don't know what to do, worship through the questions — “is my faith really real? God knows.” The unanswered questions are cues to lift your eyes to the all-wise Maker.
Big ideaWhen your own understanding runs dry, the restless questions of a downcast soul are meant to be lifted into worship — because assurance rests not on how sure you feel but on the all-wise God who knows you completely and whose love for you nothing in all creation can break.
Doctrine
Assurance; the omniscience and sovereignty of God; faith seeking understanding
Anchor text
Romans 8:31–39 ("who shall separate us…?")
Form
Insomniac, reverb-drenched shoegaze — verses hushed to a whisper over the questions, then a wide, golden Lord Huron chorus where every doubt resolves into the same three words
Voices
The Sons of Korah (Psalm 42); James (the epistle, ch. 1)

Study this song

Teaching aids drawn from the song — for personal study or group discussion.

How the song moves

1
Worship through the questions
The opening counsel: "do you know what to do when you don't know what to do?" — not resolve the doubt first, but "worship through all of the questions," refusing to lean on an understanding that has run out (Proverbs 3:5–6).
2
God knows (chorus 1)
Three unanswerable questions — is my faith real, why these feelings, when will my enemies kneel — each meets the same refrain: "God knows." Assurance is grounded not in the singer's certainty but in God's, the downcast soul preaching hope back to itself (Psalm 42:5).
3
Cues to lift your eyes
Verse 2 reframes the doubt: "those questions are just cues to lift your eyes to the maker of the skies, to the one who's all wise" — the mirror-questions of chorus 2 become an invitation to ask wisdom of the God who gives it freely (James 1:5).
4
No one (verse 3 + outro)
Caught "tossin on your bed," the song says "worship instead," then turns Paul's rhetorical questions into a chant — who can stand against His will, break the bond He sealed, take the love He feels toward you? No one (Romans 8:38–39).

Key terms & allusions

  • "Is my faith really real? God knows." — The song's title-move rests assurance on God's omniscience rather than the believer's self-scrutiny. It echoes Peter's answer to Jesus' thrice-asked "do you love me?" — "Lord, you know everything; you know that I love you" (John 21:17) — and 2 Timothy 2:19, "the Lord knows those who are his." When you cannot read your own heart, He can.
  • "worship through all of the questions" / "why do I have these feels" — A near-restatement of Psalm 42:5 (the Sons of Korah): "Why are you cast down, O my soul… Hope in God." The psalmist argues with his own emotions instead of obeying them — the pattern later Reformed writers called preaching to yourself rather than merely listening to yourself. The song's whole strategy is to worship before the feelings resolve.
  • "when you don't know what to do" / lean not on your own understanding — The counsel behind the verses is Proverbs 3:5–6: "Trust in the LORD with all your heart, and do not lean on your own understanding… and he will make straight your paths." The Hebrew binah (understanding) is exactly the faculty that fails you in the dark; the proverb tells you to trust rather than to figure it out.
  • "the maker of the skies… the one who's all wise" — James 1:5 — "If any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask God, who gives generously to all without reproach." The doubting questions are recast not as failures but as "cues" to petition the God who does not shame the asker, whose wisdom made the very skies (cf. Proverbs 3:19).
  • "Who can stand against His will? … Who can take the love he feels toward you? No one." — The outro re-verses Romans 8:31–39: "If God is for us, who can be against us?" and "who shall separate us from the love of Christ?" The "bond He sealed" points to the Spirit's sealing (Ephesians 1:13); the answered longing "when will my enemies kneel" looks to the day every knee bows (Philippians 2:10). Paul's questions are unanswerable on purpose — the answer is always "no one."

Discussion questions

  1. The chorus answers every hard question with "God knows" instead of an answer. When has "God knows" landed on you as genuine comfort, and when has it felt like a cop-out — and what makes the difference between the two?
  2. In Psalm 42 the psalmist argues with his own soul: "Why are you cast down?" How can we as a group actually help one another "worship through the questions" rather than wait for every question to be resolved first?
  3. The outro turns Paul's questions in Romans 8 into a chant — who can stand against, break the bond, take the love: no one. How does grounding assurance in God's knowledge and grip (rather than our own certainty) change the way we care for a brother or sister who is doubting?

For contemplation

  1. Which of the song's questions is actually yours right now — "is my faith really real," "who's this in the mirror," "am I further or nearer"? Can you hand that exact question to the God who already knows the answer (John 21:17)?
  2. Verse 3 catches you "tossin on your bed" and says "worship instead." What is the 3 a.m. question that keeps you awake, and what would it look like tonight to lift your eyes rather than lean on your own understanding (Proverbs 3:5–6)?
  3. The song insists nothing can take "the love he feels toward you." Does your standing rest on His grip or on your feelings — and where do you most need Romans 8:38–39 to be truer than the face in your mirror (Romans 8:38–39)?
