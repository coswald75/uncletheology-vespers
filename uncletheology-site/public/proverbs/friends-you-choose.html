Verse 1

I was thirteen with a ratty backpack,
Walkman headphones blasting track-by-track.
Thought the kids who broke the rules
were the embodiment of cool.
But Proverbs read me like a bio,
wisdom hit me like a ton of bricks:
the wrong crowd’ll wreck your tomorrow —
wisdom’s first lesson is simply this.

Chorus

The first lesson in the book

the ones you run with
shape how your story ends.
And the friends you didn’t choose
(yeah, mom and dad came standard-issue),
turns out the ones who raised you
are the ones who help you choose
who you let inside your ride,
who you hand your keys to.

soon as trouble came my way.

not two-face smiles when they walk away.
Better honest wounds than sweet pretend —
yeah, I learned that the harder way.

Study this song

Teaching aids drawn from the song — for personal study or group discussion.

How the song moves

1
The wrong crowd looks cool
At thirteen the rule-breakers looked like the embodiment of cool — till Proverbs “read me like a bio” (Proverbs 1:10–19).
2
Walk with the wise
Run with fools and you’ll be broken; walk with the wise and you’ll ascend (Proverbs 13:20).
3
Standard-issue family
The friends you didn’t choose — parents, siblings — had the vantage to protect you from the goofs (Proverbs 1:8).
4
Faithful wounds
Real friends sharpen, they don’t dull you; “better honest wounds than sweet pretend” (Proverbs 27:6, 17).

Key terms & allusions

  • “walk with the wise / companion of fools” — Proverbs 13:20, the song’s backbone: “whoever walks with the wise becomes wise, but the companion of fools will suffer harm.”
  • “faithful are the wounds of a friend” — Proverbs 27:6 — “better honest wounds than sweet pretend”; the friend’s honest wound vs. the enemy’s profuse kisses.
  • “iron sharpens iron” — Proverbs 27:17, behind “real friends sharpen, never dull you.”
  • “my son, hear… do not walk with them” — Proverbs 1:8–19 — the father’s opening plea against being enticed by bad company.
  • “ride-or-dies went zero” — The fair-weather friend exposed the moment trouble comes (cf. Proverbs 18:24).

Discussion questions

  1. The song says the friends you didn’t choose (family) help you choose the ones you do. How has that worked — or not — in your story?
  2. “Better honest wounds than sweet pretend.” When did a friend’s hard truth help you? When have you withheld one?
  3. How do you tell a “ride-or-die” from a fair-weather friend before the storm hits?

For contemplation

  1. Who are you “walking with” — and where are they leading you?
  2. Is there a faithful wound you’ve been resenting that you should actually receive?
  3. Are you the kind of friend who sharpens, or one who just flatters?
