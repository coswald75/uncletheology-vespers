Study this song

Teaching aids drawn from the song — for personal study or group discussion.

How the song moves

1
Once for all
Contend for the faith delivered to the saints; if you drift, you don’t upgrade truth — you abandon the anchor meant to keep you (Jude 3).
2
The hall of warnings
Egypt, Sodom, Cain’s envy, Balaam’s greed, Korah’s pride — “clouds with no rain, wandering stars” (Jude 5–13).
3
Michael’s restraint
Even the archangel didn’t revile but said “The Lord rebuke you”; truth doesn’t scream when the Judge is near (Jude 9).
4
Kept by mercy
Build yourselves up, pray in the Spirit, show mercy; He who began it will keep you blameless before His glory (Jude 20–24).

Key terms & allusions

  • “once for all delivered” — Jude 3 — the faith as a fixed deposit handed down, not an evolving thing to be upgraded.
  • Cain, Balaam, Korah — Jude’s three archetypes of apostasy: murderous envy (Genesis 4), the prophet-for-hire’s greed (Numbers 22–24), and rebellion against God-given authority (Numbers 16).
  • “wandering stars / clouds without rain” — Jude 12–13 — vivid images of teachers who promise everything and deliver nothing.
  • “The Lord rebuke you” — Michael the archangel’s words, disputing over the body of Moses (Jude 9; cf. Zechariah 3:2) — restraint instead of reviling.
  • “able to keep you from stumbling” — Jude 24 — the great closing doxology that anchors the chorus.

Discussion questions

  1. Jude says to “contend” for the faith and also to “show mercy.” How do you hold earnest defense and mercy together?
  2. Cain, Balaam, Korah — envy, greed, pride. Which is most at work in the church today, and how?
  3. The chorus rests on God keeping us, not us keeping ourselves. How should that change the tone of “contending”?

For contemplation

  1. Where are you tempted to “upgrade truth” rather than admit you’ve drifted?
  2. Of Cain (envy), Balaam (greed), Korah (pride) — which most exposes your heart right now?
  3. Can you rest today in “kept by mercy, not by fear”?
