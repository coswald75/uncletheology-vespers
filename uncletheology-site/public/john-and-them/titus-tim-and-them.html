Study this song

Teaching aids drawn from the song — for personal study or group discussion.

How the song moves

1
Why a Jude tune here
Jude “zooms in on false teachers so close you can see they pores”; they creep in unnoticed, twisting grace into a license (Jude 4).
2
Guard the deposit
Titus says keep doctrine tight; they profess to know God but deny Him in their works (Titus 1:16).
3
Godliness for gain
1 Timothy 6 exposes the love of money dressed as ministry — a “form of godliness” with no power (1 Timothy 6:5; 2 Timothy 3:5).
4
Itching ears
2 Timothy 4: people gather teachers who say what they want to hear; same cross denied, different century (2 Timothy 4:3–4).

Key terms & allusions

  • “Jude verse four” — The hinge text: “certain people have crept in unnoticed… who pervert the grace of our God into sensuality.”
  • “a form of godliness” — 2 Timothy 3:5 — “having the appearance of godliness but denying its power.”
  • “itching ears” — 2 Timothy 4:3 — hearers who accumulate teachers “to suit their own passions.”
  • godliness for profit — 1 Timothy 6:5 — those who imagine “godliness is a means of gain.”
  • Titus and Tim — Paul’s pastoral letters to Titus (on Crete) and Timothy (at Ephesus) — the field manuals for guarding sound doctrine.

Discussion questions

  1. The song says false teachers “smile wide, shake hands… enslave you with plans.” How do you spot the polite, plausible counterfeit?
  2. “Itching ears gather teachers who say what you wanna hear.” How do our own desires shape who we choose to listen to?
  3. What’s the difference between healthy discernment and a critical, fault-finding spirit?

For contemplation

  1. Whose teaching are you drawn to mainly because it tells you what you already want to hear?
  2. Where might you be tempted to “lighten the load” of a hard truth?
  3. Is there any way you treat godliness as “a means of gain”?
