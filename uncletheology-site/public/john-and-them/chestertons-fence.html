Study this song

Teaching aids drawn from the song — for personal study or group discussion.

How the song moves

1
The fence parable
Chesterton: don’t remove a fence till you know why it was built. The cross looks weak till grace explodes in resurrection light (Jude 3).
2
Don’t cut the wire
You ask why it’s there before you decide; the gospel’s fence is amazing grace, not a flaw to fix or a system to mend (1 John 2:24).
3
Chronological snobbery
“Clive” warns that truth doesn’t age — mocking the past as obsolete is itself a fashion that will rot (Hebrews 13:8).
4
No new Christs
Same Christ reigning from start to end; it needs no edits from modern man (Proverbs 22:28).

Key terms & allusions

  • Chesterton’s fence — From G.K. Chesterton’s The Thing (1929): before you tear down a fence whose purpose you can’t see, first find out why it was put there. A rule against reckless “reform.”
  • “Clive” — C.S. Lewis — Clive Staples Lewis — pictured “in the corner, pipe smoke in the air.”
  • chronological snobbery — A phrase Lewis credits to Owen Barfield (in Surprised by Joy): the uncritical assumption that whatever is newer is truer, and the old is discredited simply for being old.
  • “ancient landmark” — Proverbs 22:28, “do not move the ancient landmark your fathers have set,” underlies the whole don’t-move-the-fence theme.
  • “No new Christs, the original is winning” — The hook — a refrain this song shares with “AntiChrist” later on the album.

Discussion questions

  1. Where in church life do people “tear down the fence” without first asking why it was built?
  2. Lewis’s “chronological snobbery” — where does our culture assume new = true? Where does the church?
  3. How do you tell a fence worth keeping from one worth removing?

For contemplation

  1. What conviction or practice have you been tempted to discard mainly because it feels old or restrictive?
  2. Have you ever “bled out later asking, who knew?” — removed a guardrail and regretted it?
  3. Where do you need to trust that “you don’t improve perfection”?
