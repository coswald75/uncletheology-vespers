Study this song

Teaching aids drawn from the song — for personal study or group discussion.

How the song moves

1
The height, stated first
Verses 1–2 pile up the glory of Hebrews 1 — heir of all, radiance, 'exact print,' upholding all things by a word, with 'no floor beneath' Him but His own speech. The fall only lands if the height is felt.
2
The dive
'Dove!' — the pre-chorus is the plunge. The chorus counts the descent in steps: lower than angels, than the sky, than the clay He formed. Salvation is measured in how far down it went.
3
Really flesh
Verse 3 refuses every shortcut: 'not a robe put on,' but bone and thirst and dust, until 'the hands that hold the stars were held.' The mantra closes it — 'not ashamed to call them brothers.'

Key terms & allusions

  • The diver (C. S. Lewis) — In Miracles, Lewis pictures the Incarnation as a diver plunging from the light down to the cold sea-bed and rising with 'the dripping, precious thing.' This song is that dive; the album's title hides the pun — the Driver is the Diver.
  • 'Exact print' — Hebrews 1:3, charaktēr — the exact imprint or stamp of God's very being, the way a seal leaves its perfect impression. The Son is not a copy but the impress of God.
  • 'Upholding by a word' — Hebrews 1:3 — creation has 'no floor beneath' it but the ongoing speech of the Son. The One holding everything up is the One who lay down in it.
  • 'Not a robe put on' — A guard against docetism — the ancient error that Christ only seemed human, wearing flesh like a costume. The song insists on real bone, thirst, sweat, and death.
  • 'Not ashamed to call them brothers' — Hebrews 2:11 — the astonishing result of the dive. The One who went to the bottom surfaces claiming us as family.

Discussion questions

  1. The song spends two verses on Christ's height before the descent. Why does the glory have to be established before the humbling means anything?
  2. 'Not a robe put on.' Why does it matter, practically and pastorally, that Jesus really had flesh, thirst, and death — not the appearance of them?
  3. Lewis's diver goes down alone into the cold to bring something back up. What is the 'dripping, precious thing' the Diver surfaces holding — and how do the next two tracks answer that?

For contemplation

  1. 'The hands that hold the stars were held.' Whose hands held them? Sit in the scene — the incarnate God, held.
  2. He is 'not ashamed' to call you brother or sister. Where do you still expect Him to be ashamed of you?
