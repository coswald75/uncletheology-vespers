Study this song

Teaching aids drawn from the song — for personal study or group discussion.

How the song moves

1
The fake dime
A word to Timothy: the order of authority is creational, not cultural — Adam formed first, Eve deceived (1 Timothy 2:12–14; Genesis 2:7).
2
Why Adam fell
The fathers’ reading: Adam ate from fear of the “lonesome load” of losing his bride — putting wife above Maker. An ordered love made foul (Genesis 3:6; Matthew 10:37).
3
The second-Adam scene
In Gethsemane, Christ loved the bride but loved the Father first, and refused the serpent’s wilderness offer to “skip the cross” (Matthew 4:8–10; John 10:18).
4
Dominion restored
Because He wouldn’t trade, He walked out of the tomb “with the keys and the bride” — true dominion bought back at a high price (Revelation 1:18; Ephesians 5:25–27).

Key terms & allusions

  • true dominion — The creational mandate to guard and keep, given in the garden (Genesis 1–2; Genesis 2:15).
  • fake dime — The counterfeit trade — surrendering a God-given calling to secure a lesser love.
  • ordered love foul — Augustine’s ordo amoris: loving good things in the wrong order. Loving wife above God disorders the love (Matthew 10:37).
  • second Adam — Christ as the last Adam who succeeds where the first failed (1 Corinthians 15:45; Romans 5:14).
  • “Get behind Me” — Christ’s rebuke of the satanic shortcut that would skip the cross (Matthew 4:10; 16:23).

Discussion questions

  1. The song says a genuinely good love (Adam’s for Eve) became idolatry when it outranked his love for God. How can a good thing become a “fake dime”?
  2. How does Christ’s refusal of the serpent’s shortcut reframe what faithful leadership actually costs?
  3. Where does our culture pressure people to “trade dominion” — to abdicate God-given responsibility — and call it wisdom?

For contemplation

  1. Where are you tempted to trade a God-given responsibility to keep a relationship or comfort — to take “a fake dime”?
  2. “A man who’d sell his God to keep his bride will lose them both.” Where do you need to love God first in order to truly love others?
  3. Check the pocket, brother — is it true dominion, or a counterfeit dime?
